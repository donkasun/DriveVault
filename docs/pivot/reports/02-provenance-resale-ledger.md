# Pivot 02: Vehicle Provenance / Resale Ledger ("Carfax for Sri Lanka")

---

## One-line positioning

A tamper-evident, shareable vehicle history ledger that helps Sri Lankan used-car sellers command a higher price on ikman and Riyasewana — and gives buyers the confidence to pay it.

---

## Why this avoids competing with CRLO

CRLO is a private logbook: records exist only for the current owner and are structurally deleted on sale (the seller unlinks the vehicle; the buyer starts from zero). This "trust cliff" at point of transfer is CRLO's structural failure mode and the exact problem this pivot exploits. CRLO also has no Sri Lanka-specific data integrations, no shareable buyer link, and no resale-value framing. This pivot is not a logbook — it is a provenance credential and a trust product, a category CRLO explicitly does not occupy.

## Market evidence (verified)

**Used-car market volume:**
Sri Lanka's used car market was valued at **USD 283 million** in 2025 and is forecast to reach USD 436 million by 2031 at a 7.49% CAGR. ([Mordor Intelligence, Sri Lanka Used Car Market](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/sri-lanka-used-car-market))

**Platform scale:**
- ikman.lk lists **78,494+ vehicles** at any given time, with **6,100+ cars actively listed** in a single July 2025 snapshot. ([ikman blog, July 2025](https://blog.ikman.lk/en/sri-lanka-car-market-2025-insights/))
- Riyasewana.com lists **16,434+ car ads** concurrently. ([Riyasewana search, accessed 2025](https://riyasewana.com/search/cars))
- In January 2024, ikman expanded its automobile verification service; in March 2024, it launched an AI price prediction tool. ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/sri-lanka-used-car-market)) — market platforms are actively investing in trust features, validating buyer demand.

**Market structure amplifies the trust problem:**
- **85.06%** of Sri Lanka's used car market is unorganized (private sellers, not dealers). ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/sri-lanka-used-car-market))
- **58.41%** of resold vehicles have had multiple previous owners. ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/sri-lanka-used-car-market))
- Hatchbacks lead at 47.14% of listings; Toyota dominates with 1,807 listings per July 2025 snapshot; Japanese brands represent 68% of inventory — all vehicles where documented service history (dealership or otherwise) can be verified or cross-referenced. ([ikman blog](https://blog.ikman.lk/en/sri-lanka-car-market-2025-insights/))

**Existing inspection services validate the trust gap:**
A clutch of professional pre-purchase inspection services already exists in Sri Lanka — Greasemonkey Inspectors (John Keells Holdings-backed, 250+ checkpoints), CarChecks, MyMechanic, SGS Sri Lanka, Automob Lanka, Open Bonnet, Drive Hub — indicating that buyers are willing to pay for third-party verification. None of them provide a persistent, transferable, seller-owned history record; they produce a one-time report that the buyer receives but the seller cannot reuse for the next sale.

**Carfax analogue as proof of concept:**
In the United States, CARFAX generates **USD 614.8 million in annual revenue** from vehicle history reports ($39.99–$99.99 per consumer report; subscription packages for dealers). ([IncFact / Carfax company data](https://incfact.com/company/carfax-centreville-va/); [AboutCarsReviews](https://aboutcarsreviews.com/articles/unveiling-the-ingenious-carfax-business-model-revolutionizing-the-automotive-industry-with-unmatched-vehicle-history-reports/)) Carfax's moat is government data integration at scale. In Sri Lanka, no such government-integrated history product exists; the moat here is the seller-maintained ledger that accumulates over ownership.

**Odometer fraud context:**
Globally, Carfax estimated ~2.45 million cars on U.S. roads may have rolled-back odometers — a 14% increase in 2025. ([Carfax, cited in KOMO News, 2025](https://komonews.com/news/local/odometer-fraud-risk-for-consumers-car-history-report-car-miles-carfax-blue-driver-checkbook-state-attorney-general-office)) Consumers lose an average of $3,300 in value from unknowingly buying a car with a rolled-back odometer. In Sri Lanka, where 85% of the market is private sellers with no regulated disclosure requirements, the problem is structurally identical without any data solution.

**Digital penetration:**
12.4 million internet users, 8.20 million Facebook users. ([DataReportal Digital 2025 Sri Lanka](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-sri-lanka)) The Sri Lankan used-car buyer is already online — ikman and Riyasewana are the primary discovery channels. The shareable buyer link can be embedded directly in a vehicle listing ad.

## Target user & Sri Lanka relevance

**Primary seller persona:** Private used-car owner aged 30–55 selling one vehicle every 2–5 years on ikman or Riyasewana. They have done routine maintenance but have no easy way to prove it to a skeptical buyer, and they are negotiated down by Rs. 50,000–200,000+ because the buyer "has no way to know." The DriveVault ledger is their evidence, and the shareable link is their listing differentiator.

**Primary buyer persona:** Colombo-region professional aged 25–45, buying a second-hand car for Rs. 1.5–5 million, who cannot afford a lemon and is willing to pay a small premium for documented history. The pre-purchase inspection industry (Greasemonkey, MyMechanic etc.) already proves this willingness to pay.

**Sri Lanka specificity:** The market's 85% private-seller composition and 58% multi-owner vehicle rate make trust the dominant purchase blocker. In a market with organized dealers or Carfax-style government data integration, this gap doesn't exist at this scale.

## User acquisition

**Embed in the sales listing workflow (zero-friction):**
The highest-leverage move is embedding the "share my DriveVault history" link directly in the seller's ikman/Riyasewana listing description. This gives DriveVault brand impressions from every buyer who clicks a link in an active listing — organic, free, at marketplace scale. Sellers are motivated to add this because it differentiates their ad.

**Facebook car buy/sell groups (8+ large groups identified):**
Groups like "All Vehicles for Sale in Sri Lanka," "Vehicle Sale Sri Lanka," "Car Sale – Sri Lanka" aggregate tens of thousands of buyers and sellers. A seller who shares their DriveVault history link in their group post exposes the brand to every interested buyer. This is pull-based, not push-based.

**AutoLanka Forum:**
Post a real case study thread — "I got Rs. 75,000 more for my Vitz because I shared my full service history on DriveVault." The AutoLanka community (Sri Lanka's largest automotive forum, active since 2011+) is exactly the early-adopter segment that will experiment and advocate.

**TikTok / YouTube Sinhala content:**
"How to prove your car's real history when selling on ikman" is a content angle that addresses a real buyer anxiety. Organic Sinhala-language content on TikTok (5.79M adult users) and YouTube (8.13M users) can drive installs with no ad spend.

**Partnership with inspection services:**
Greasemonkey, MyMechanic, and SGS Sri Lanka already serve the verification-hungry segment. A cross-referral arrangement — "your Greasemonkey inspection report is automatically added to your DriveVault ledger" — gives both parties incremental value. No exclusivity needed; frame as "add inspection to your history."

**What to do first:** Post to 3 Facebook car-sale groups explaining the concept with a real shareable link example. Measure clicks vs. app installs. Before spending money, validate that buyers actually open the shared link.

## Virality

**The built-in loop — the shareable buyer link:**
Every time a DriveVault seller posts their vehicle for sale, they attach a unique read-only link: `drivevault.lk/v/abc123`. The buyer sees the full maintenance timeline, fuel and odometer history, document expiry records, and any inspection reports attached — without needing an account. This is the artifact that gets shared and why:

1. Seller includes DriveVault link in their ikman/Riyasewana listing.
2. Every buyer who clicks the link sees the DriveVault brand before seeing the history.
3. Buyers who are impressed convert to sellers when they eventually list their own car, wanting the same trust signal.
4. The K-factor is structural: each listing link is a passive brand impression on an unknown number of buyers (each listing gets viewed dozens to hundreds of times before a sale).

**The "premium seller" identity loop:**
In community Facebook groups and forums, sellers who share documented history gain status as "serious sellers." This social signal encourages other sellers to adopt DriveVault to avoid looking like they're hiding something.

**The inspection attachment loop:**
When a buyer commissions a Greasemonkey or MyMechanic inspection, the inspector attaches the report to the vehicle's DriveVault record. The next buyer of the same vehicle now sees that history — creating a chain of trust across ownership transfers, where each inspection permanently enriches the record.

## Revenue model & potential

**Model:** Seller-side freemium. History ledger and basic sharing are free. The seller pays for the "Verified Listing" badge or enhanced share page.

**Pricing tiers:**

| Tier | Price | What it includes |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Rs. 0 | Full history ledger, unlimited shareable links, no badge |
| Verified Share | Rs. 490 one-time per vehicle | "DriveVault Verified" badge on share page, inspection integration slot, PDF export |
| Pro Seller | Rs. 1,490/year | Unlimited vehicles, badge on all, priority listing link in ikman/Riyasewana integrations (if partnership achieved) |
| Inspection Partner API | Revenue share | Inspection companies pay per report injected into DriveVault record |

**The one-time "Verified Share" at Rs. 490 is the primary monetization moment:** it is triggered exactly when the car is listed for sale — the highest motivation moment. The seller has just decided to sell; Rs. 490 is trivial vs. a potential Rs. 50,000–200,000 negotiation loss from undocumented history.

**TAM math:**

- Sri Lanka used-car market: USD 283M in 2025 ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/sri-lanka-used-car-market))
- Estimated private-seller transaction volume (85% of market at avg ~USD 4,000/car): ~60,000 private-seller transactions/year
- At Rs. 490 per verified listing: if 10% of sellers pay = 6,000 transactions × Rs. 490 = **Rs. 2.94 million/year**
- At 25% conversion (realistic once "DriveVault Verified" has social proof): 15,000 × Rs. 490 = **Rs. 7.35 million/year**

**B2B revenue (Year 2+):**
Pre-purchase inspection services (Greasemonkey, SGS, MyMechanic) charge Rs. 2,500–6,000+ per inspection. A per-report API fee of Rs. 200–400 for attaching inspection results to a DriveVault record is high-margin, requires no consumer acquisition by DriveVault, and is a natural distribution partnership. 10,000 inspections/year × Rs. 300 = **Rs. 3 million/year** incremental.

**Conservative Year 2 total:** Rs. 3–6 million/year (verified listings + API)
**Moderate Year 3 total:** Rs. 10–15 million/year (seller adoption + B2B inspection integrations + dealer tier)

**Dealer tier (Year 2+):** Used-car dealers (organized 14.94% of market, growing at 9.78% CAGR) have strong incentive to differentiate on history. A dealer subscription at Rs. 5,000/month for unlimited listings = Rs. 60,000/year/dealer. Acquiring 20 dealers = Rs. 1.2 million/year.

## Feature roadmap by phase

### Phase 1 / MVP

*Already in DriveVault (use as-is or minor adaptation):*
- Multi-vehicle garage with photos (Cloudinary)
- Fuel logs with odometer readings (timestamped, immutable once saved)
- Expense/maintenance logs
- Document vault with expiry dates (insurance, fitness, revenue licence, emission)
- Firebase Auth, Cloud Run backend

*Net-new for MVP:*
- **Shareable read-only vehicle page:** public URL (`/v/{share_token}`) requiring no login, showing odometer timeline, maintenance events, document expiry history, and photos
- **Share token generation:** backend endpoint to create/revoke a share link per vehicle
- **"This record was created by the owner, not editable by DriveVault" trust statement** on share page (tamper-evident by design — log entries carry created_at timestamps from the server)
- **Basic timeline view** on share page: chronological list of fuel logs, expenses, document events
- **Copy link / share sheet** in the mobile app for the seller to post in their listing

### Phase 2

- **Inspection report attachment:** API endpoint for partner inspection services to POST a structured report to a vehicle record; displayed as a verified block on the share page
- **Verified Listing badge:** in-app purchase flow (Rs. 490) that upgrades the share page to show a trust badge and enables PDF export
- **Odometer consistency check:** flag if a fuel log odometer reading is lower than a previous one (tamper-signal for sellers to investigate before listing)
- **QR code for share page:** attach to vehicle windscreen during test drives or physical auctions
- **Ownership transfer flow:** seller "hands off" the vehicle in-app; history persists but new owner's chapter begins; both parties get a transfer confirmation

### Phase 3

- **Dealer dashboard:** multi-vehicle inventory view with bulk history management for used-car lots
- **Price estimator:** "vehicles with DriveVault Verified history sell for X% more than unlisted equivalents" — computed from anonymized platform data and quoted to the seller
- **OCR for receipts and service records:** ML Kit scan of a garage receipt auto-populates a maintenance event (Phase 3 per existing tech spec)
- **ikman / Riyasewana listing integration:** embed DriveVault link or badge natively in partner listings via API (requires commercial negotiation)
- **Expiry-to-resale funnel:** when a user's vehicle insurance is about to expire and the vehicle is >5 years old, offer a "time to sell?" prompt with a pre-built share link — cross-pivot synergy with the Compliance Guardian

## Risks & moat

**Top risks:**

1. **Adoption chicken-and-egg:** Buyers won't value the share link until sellers use it; sellers won't pay for the badge until buyers demand it. The market must be seeded — early adopters need to visibly get better outcomes before word spreads. Mitigate: make the free share link genuinely useful (no paywall on viewing), drive early seller adoption through enthusiast communities, and create explicit "DriveVault Verified" marketing materials sellers can share organically.

2. **ikman / Riyasewana could build this natively:** ikman already launched a verification service (Jan 2024) and AI price tool (March 2024). If they build a seller-maintained history ledger natively, they have an enormous distribution advantage. Mitigate: move fast to establish the DriveVault brand as the independent third-party, which is more trusted than a marketplace-owned history (same reason Carfax is trusted over dealer-provided reports). Pursue inspection-service partnerships as a distribution moat before platforms replicate.

3. **Data falsification by sellers:** A seller can enter false maintenance records. Mitigate: (a) server-side timestamps make retroactive insertion obvious on the timeline; (b) third-party inspection reports (Greasemonkey etc.) are injected via API, not editable by the seller; (c) the share page clearly distinguishes "owner-entered" vs. "inspection-verified" events.

4. **Low transaction frequency:** A typical SL car owner sells a vehicle once every 3–7 years. This means the monetization event (Verified Listing Rs. 490) is infrequent. Mitigate: the daily utility is the compliance and maintenance logging (Pivot 1 overlap), which keeps users active and builds the history between sale events.

**Moat:**

- **Accumulated history depth:** The longer a user keeps records in DriveVault, the richer and more valuable their vehicle's history ledger becomes. Switching to a competitor means starting from zero — a high switching cost that compounds over time.
- **Inspection partner integrations:** Once 2–3 inspection services pipe their reports into DriveVault, the share page gains third-party-verified content that competitors cannot replicate without the same partnerships.
- **Network effect via share links:** Each share link that appears in an ikman listing is a free brand impression on buyers who may become future sellers. The pool of DriveVault-verified vehicles grows passively with every sale, making the brand association "this is what a trustworthy seller does."
- **Independence as a trust signal:** A seller-owned history maintained in a third-party app is more credible than a dealer-maintained record or a marketplace-native tool. This is the same structural advantage Carfax has over manufacturer or dealer history.

## Viability verdict

| Dimension | Score /10 | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition ease | **6** | Seller motivation is real but infrequent (sells once in 3–7 years); daily utility must drive retention between sale events; Facebook + community channels reduce CAC but seeding takes longer |
| Virality | **9** | The shareable buyer link is a genuine built-in distribution mechanism — every active listing with a DriveVault link is an impression on an unknown number of buyers; structurally strong |
| Monetization | **5** | One-time per-listing fee model has low transaction frequency; B2B inspection API and dealer subscriptions lift ceiling but require more negotiation and build effort; Rs. 3–15M/year realistic by Year 3 |
| Defensibility | **9** | Accumulated history depth + inspection partner integrations + independence-as-trust-signal create compounding moats; platform risk (ikman building natively) is the primary threat but manageable via speed and partnerships |
| Build effort | **6** | Shareable read-only page + share token backend is moderate net-new work; tamper-evident design and ownership transfer add complexity; inspection partner API is a meaningful integration effort; 50–60% of infrastructure reused from DriveVault Phase 1 |

**Bottom line:** The Provenance Ledger has the strongest virality mechanic of any DriveVault pivot — the shareable buyer link creates organic distribution at marketplace scale with zero ad spend — but monetization is structurally tied to an infrequent event (vehicle sale), which limits revenue ceiling and makes daily utility retention critical. Best pursued as a second phase after or alongside the Compliance Guardian, which provides the daily engagement loop that keeps the history ledger alive between sale events.
